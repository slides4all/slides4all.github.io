How to give a great talk

A talk exists so that the audience understands your argument and follows your reasoning. If they did not, the talk failed, no matter how good the work behind it was.

This site collects what works in practice: how to build the slides, how to rehearse, how to use the time you were given, and how to review everything before sending it. Every recommendation came out of real reviews of accepted conference talks, and almost all of them began as a mistake that kept repeating.

There is no theory here. There is a set of decisions that improve almost anyone's talk, a gallery of complete examples to copy and adapt, and a checklist to run before you send your slides.

If you only have five minutes

These five decisions solve most of the problems that show up in reviews.

  • Make the type bigger. It is by far the most common problem. Assume a large, full room.
  • Open your slides on a phone, 50 cm from your eyes. Whatever you cannot read, the audience cannot either.
  • When space runs out, cut content. Never shrink the type.
  • White background, black text. No gray, no color behind text.
  • Rehearse five times, standing and out loud. The delivery only flows from the fifth run onward.

This site follows the rules it teaches: white background, large type, little color and no ornament. It passes the phone test too.

Where this material comes from

The starting point was the presentation guidance page of SBSeg 2024, a Brazilian security and privacy symposium, written by a group of researchers for the authors of accepted papers. To that were added the review notes, the summary of suggestions and the checklist produced in 2026 out of dozens of talks reviewed one by one.
